The ar rival of the absent members will render the voice of that branch un certain on the point of a Convention. The decision of Massachusetts either way will involve the result in this State. The minority in Penna, is very restless under their defeat. If they can get an Assembly to their wish they will endeavor to undermine what has been done there. If backed by Massts. they will probably be emboldened to make some more rash experiment. The information from Georgia continues to be favorable. The little we get from S. Carolina is of the same complexion. If I am not misinformed as to the arrival of some members for Con gress, a quorum is at length made up. With the most perfect esteem & attachment, I remain Dear Sir, Your Obedt. humble servant, Js. Madison Jr. RC (DLC: Washington Papers). Madison, Papers (Rutland), 10:399.
