Thomasjefferson on behalf of the state, see ibid., p. 17n.4. Elias Boudinot to George Washington Dear Sir, Philadelphia May 7th. 1783. I have duly reed, your Excellency’s several public dispatches of the 9th, 18th & 30t of Aprill and 3d May, all which have from time to time been laid before Congress. 1 We are without any farther advices from Europe than what your Ex cellency has already received, except that Compte De Vergennes in a Letter to the Minister of France of the 27th Febry,2 mentions that there are no difficulties in the Way of the definitive Treaty—that they were waiting for Answers from the Courts of Petersburgh & Vienna, as the Emperor & Empress had been invited to attend the signing of that Treaty in the Character of Mediation, I suppose as a Compliment. I shall be careful to transmit the first Intelligence we receive from Europe, without delay. I have the Honor to be with every Sentiment of regard & Esteem, Your Excellency’s Most Obedt. & very Hble Servt Elias Boudinot RC (DLC: Washington Papers). 1 These letters are in PCC, item 152, 11:211—14, 219—26, 237—40, 249—52; and Wash ington, Writings (Fitzpatrick), 26:308, 330-34, 370, 398-400. 2 Vergennes’ February 27 letter to the chevalier de La Luzerne is in Archives du Min istere des affaires etrangeres: Correspondence politique, Etats-Unis, 23:188—90.
