James Monroe to James Madison Dear Sir Phila. 1 Sepr. 25. 1786. I arriv’d here the evening of the day I left you & found Mrs. Monroe & the family well. They desire to be remember’d to you. I think you mention’d you had not reed, the letter I had address’d you in answer to yours before you left Phila., the last from that place. 2 You express’d an uneasiness at failing to command such a sum, as might enable you to furnish me with the sum I advanc’d for you in the affr. of Taylor. 3 I then desir’d & again repeat it, that you give yourself no anxiety upon this subject. Had it been convenient, & perfectly so, to remit it any time be fore I left this place it wod. been well—but I shod, have been unwilling & beg of you to act accordingly, to receive it, if it shod, expose you to the smallest inconvenience. I shall be able so to arrange my affrs. here as to accomodate your payment to me in Virga. & sometime hence to suit me. I have just taken my seat in Congress & know little of their pro ceedings. I shall write you again & constantly whilst you remn. in Phila. Make my best respects to Mrs. Trist & the family & be assur’d of the sin cerity with which I am yr. friend & servant, Jas.
