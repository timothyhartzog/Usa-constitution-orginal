Charles Thomson to Thomas Lawrence Sir, Office of Secretary of Congress, Aug. 25. 1786 I have received and had the honor of communicating to Congress your letter of the 24, giving information in behalf of Mr W Morris that one of the certificates mentioned in his Memorial has been discov ered. 1 Having nothing in charge on this subject I herewith return the certificate and am, Sr, Your obedient humble Servt. LB (DNA: PCC, item 18B). 1 William Walton Morris, “late a Lieut, in the second Regiment of Artillery,” had peti tioned June 20, 1786, for the replacement of “Commutation Certificates” issued to him by Paymaster John Pierce which he lost with his wallet while crossing a river. PCC, item 42, 5:383-86. His petition had been referred to the board of treasury, whose August 5 report had recommended against the reissuance of such certificates without tangible documentation, although no action had been taken upon the report. JCC, 31:498-501. Lawrence’s August 24 letter simply reported that one of the certificates claimed by Mor ris was found to have been paid, a discovery that would have rendered the claim moot had not the treasury already recommended against it. PCC, item 78, 14:731-34. Lawrence was a New York merchant operating in partnership with Jacob Morris at 83 Broadway. Hamilton, Papers (Syrett), 3:673.
