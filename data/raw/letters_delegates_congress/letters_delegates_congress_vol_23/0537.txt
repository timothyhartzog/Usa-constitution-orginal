James Monroe to Philip Mazzei Dear Sir New York Augt. 19. 1786. I have reed, the several letters mentiond in your last enclosing a power of Attorney in French to act for you in the affair of Mr Dohrman & thank you for the contents. At present 1 have only time to write you upon that particular subject and to assure you that Mr. Dohrman is here, 1 and perfectly dispos’d to do every thing in his power to remedy the injuries you have sustain’d by his engagments. I send you a letter from him on that subject—he has been most morti fied & to all appearance distress’d beyond measure upon this subject. His claims agnst. the publick are now in the hands of the treasury Board, so soon as they shall report & Congress admit them to any accot. agnst the publick he will give me such assurance as will arrest it there for you—but when you will afterwards get it is very incertain— not shortly I am well assur’d. If you shod, not before I leave this which will be in Octr. next I shall leave the charge of this business in the hands either of Colo. Grayson or Carrington of which however I will inform you. Make my particular regards to Short—assure him of my best wishes—That my friends when I leave Congress shall be his. Believe me sincerely yr. friend & servant, Jas.
