Written by Benson, and signed by Benson, Duane, Haring, and Smith. 1 With the Massachusetts agents’ letter of September 7, which has not been found, the territorial dispute between Massachusetts and New York that had been submitted to Congress for federal determination in December 1784, entered a new phase. After ex periencing repeated difficulties in attempting to select judges and convene a federal court, both sides had become impatient and the assemblies of the two states had autho rized their agents to settle the controversy “otherwise than by a Foederal Court.” Com pared to the previous pace of events, negotiations proceeded swiftly. In response to Mas sachusetts’ proposal of Newport, Hartford, and New Haven as possible sites for a conference, New York selected Hartford and proposed convening November 6, and Massachusetts responded by proposing November 30, a proposal that was accepted. For the date counterproposal, see Massachusetts Agents to the New York Agents, October 12. Addressed: “Ebenezer Hazard Esqr, P[ost] M [aster] G [en eral].” 1 For the enclosed September 20 resolve, directing the postmaster general “to receive no other money in payment for postage than specie,” see JCC, 31:674—75. By mid-1786 paper money advocates had gained control in seven states, and in Rhode Island, New jersey, and North Carolina Continental postmasters were being pressured to accept state paper for postage. Hazard had reported the problem June 19, 1786, explaining that he had directed his deputies not to receive the paper money of any state for postage, and Congress had immediately endorsed his action. But the pressures contin ued to mount, especially in North Carolina, and on September 19 Hazard reported that postmasters in Edenton and Wilmington had been threatened physically for refusing the state’s paper, and indeed that no other money was in circulation in the state. “In this Dilemma,” he groaned, “I am totally at a Loss how to act.” Acting once again with uncharacteristic speed, Congress virtually ignored Hazard’s lament and reaffirmed its previous policy.
