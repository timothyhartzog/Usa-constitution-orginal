That Lopez had come to Philadelphia to monitor the committee’s proceedings in this and a companion case involving another of his schooners is evident from the following note to him from Connecticut delegate Jesse Root, dated “Philadelphia, March 31st, 1779.” Root, who was attorney for Lopez when this case was tried in Connecticut, had himself been appointed to the Committee on Appeals on March 9, 1779, but avoided becoming formally involved in this decree. “I am called upon,” Root wrote, “to attend a Committee of Congress at five O Clock this afternoon the time I appointed to wait on you—which I knew not of when I saw you this Morning. You will be so good as to excuse my not meeting you at that time & I will attend you at any other time you will nominate.” Wetmore Papers, MHi.
