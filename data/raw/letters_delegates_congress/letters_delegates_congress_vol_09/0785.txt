Henry Laurens to Samuel Chase Dear Sir, 26 May [1778] Under Cover of your favor of the 20th I received yesterday two Packets directed to Mr. M. Ridley, Paris. 1 These come too late to go with the first class of dispatches to our Ambassadors, but tis possible to overtake them. I have sent both packets to Mr. Otis at Boston & presuming one is duplicate of the other have requested his particular care to forward them by the two first good Vessels, those in which the public papers go, if on time. I am with great esteem &ca. [P.S.] Every day bring similar accounts of the Enemy’s preparations for evacuating Philadelphia, but not a word from the General since the 18th. I had a Message yesterday from General Stiiben, he hoped to see me in Philadelphia in six days. The Marquis delafayette had an opportunity of displaying his Generalship last Week in a retreat without loss. To the honor of Baron Stiiben when the account of the Marquis’s being surrounded had reached the Camp & the Alarm fired, the whole were completely under Arms, formed &: ready to March in fifteen Minutes. LB (ScHi). 1 Matthew Ridley, a Baltimore merchant residing in France who was appointed by the Maryland legislature in 1781 as an agent to negotiate European loans for the state. Kathryn Sullivan, Maryland and France, 1774-1789 (Philadelphia: Uni versity of Pennsylvania Press, 1936), pp. 52, 121-34.
