Arnold RC (DLC: Washington Papers). 1 That is, Washington’s January 20 letter, which was read in Congress on January 27. It contained an accusation that Arnold had sent a letter to Vermont warning Luke Knowl ton and Samuel Wells of their impending arrest for a “dangerous correspondence and intercourse with the enemy,” thereby eluding Capt. Ebenezer Macomber, who had been sent by Washington to carry out the arrest. Arnold did acknowledge writing to Vermont leaders Thomas Chittenden and Jonas Fay at about that time, and promised to produce copies of the letters, but ultimately failed to, for which see Arnold to Washington, Febru ary 11, 1783. See also James Madison’s Notes of Debates, January 27; Elias Boudinot to Washington, November 27, 1782, and January 29, 1783; Washington, Papers (Fitzpatrick), 25:350-51, 394, 407-8, 26:53-54, 96, 144-45; and Walton, Records of Vermont, 2:259-60. 2 However, for a letter suggestive of Arnold’s connections with developments in Ver mont, see Arnold to Daniel Cahoon, November 4 and 15, 1782. Elias Boudinot to Benjamin Lincoln My dear Genl Philadelphia Jany 28th 1783 I was Honored with the recp 1 of your Letter of the 9th Inst pr Post—I having nothing new to Communicate and the design of this is to Comply with my promise, in Acquainting you, that, altho’ there is no immediate Business that demands your presence yet I believe if you could return By the middle of Feb y or thereabouts without any great Inconvenience to your private affairs, that it would give great sat isfaction to Congress 1 —I do not mean to hurry you, but only to hint my opinion on the appearance of things at present. Mrs Boudinot & Miss Susan return the most respectful & affectionate Compliments to Mrs Lincoln & yourself. . . . I am my Dr Sir with great Esteem, Your most Obedient & very Hble Servt, E. Boudinot.
