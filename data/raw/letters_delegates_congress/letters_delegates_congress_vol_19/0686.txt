652 February 3, 1783 Elias Boudinot to Frederick A. Muhlenberg Sir, Philadelphia, 3d February 1783. I have the honor to enclose a copy of an Act of Congress of the 30th ult. in answer to the Memorials of the Legislature of the 28th of August and 12th of November last. 1 The nature and great importance of the subjects have been the principal causes of the long delay of this Answer; but I hope it will yet serve the purposes of the Legislature and meet their expectations. 2 A misinformation of your not sitting on Saturday was the, cause of its not being sent on that day. I have the honor to be &c, E.B. LB (DNA: PCC, item 16). Addressed: “The Honorable Frederick A. Muhlenberg, Esq. Speaker of the Assembly of Pennsylvania.” 1 See JCC, 24:99-105. For the context of this congressional action, see James Madi son’s Notes of Debates, November 20, December 4, 1782, and January 30, 1783; and Morris, Papers (Ferguson), 7:143-48. 2 Although this congressional response to Pennsylvania’s plea for funding the Conti nental debt failed to meet the demands of Pennsylvania creditors seeking the payment of interest on Continental certificates, it did open the way for the Pennsylvania Assembly to adopt its own compromise program for the temporary relief of the state’s public creditors and enabled Congress to shift its attention to paying the arrears due the Continental Army. For a discussion of the Pennsylvania Assembly’s March 21 funding act (providing for a one-year assumption of interest payments to its own citizens), which gave Congress time to adopt a funding plan of its own on April 18 (although it failed to receive the nec essary unanimous ratification of the states), see Morris, Papers (Ferguson), 7:146-47. Elias Boudinot to William Livingston D. Sir, Philadelphia Febry. 4th. 1783. I had the Honor of recieving your Excellency’s Letter per Post, and agreeably to your request, enclose a Copy of the Act of Congress referred to. 1 I laid your Excellency’s Letter before Congress, who thought noth ing farther necessary, on this Occasion. We have nothing new, worthy of Communication, but am in daily expectation of important advices from Europe. I duly forwarded your Excellency’s Letter to Mr. Jefferson, 2 by Express I was sending to that Gentleman. I have the Honor to be with great Respect, Your Excellency’s Most Obedt Hble Servt.
