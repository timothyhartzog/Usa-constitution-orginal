I do not wish to flatter the Abilities of Genl. Lincoln, least he should grow too vain, & think him self really the Man alone of the Und. States. But he has been, & still is, of infinite Service to us, in his Office. He has succeeded in his Office beyond my most sanguine Expectations. I beleive for this Time, you will be very glad to lay me aside; but not before you have permitted me to add, with how much Esteem & Respect, I am, Dear Sir, Your most obedient Servant, Samuel Osgood RC (DLC: Osgood Papers). 1 Osgood added this date line after concluding the letter, which he clearly composed over a period of several days, probably on September 19, 20, and 25.
