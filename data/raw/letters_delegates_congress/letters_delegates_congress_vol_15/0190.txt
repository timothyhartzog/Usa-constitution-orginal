Samuel Huntington to Thomas Sim Lee Sir Philadelphia 19 May 1780 I am directed to acquaint you that there being but one Delegate from Maryland attending at Congress that State is not represented. That Matters of great Importance are now lying before Congress, which require the United Councils, and vigorous Exertions of all the States; and it earnestly requested that Maryland may be represented in Congress as soon as possible. 1 With great respect & Esteem, I have the honor to be, your Excellency’s hbble servt, Sam Huntington President RC (MdAA: Red Books). In a clerical hand and signed by Huntington. 1 For Congress’ May 17 resolve directing Huntington to urge Maryland and Dela ware to send a representation to Congress, see JCC, 17:435. Although several matters of importance were before Congress, this resolve was undoubtedly adopted at the request of New York, which had long been pressing for a congressional decision on the Vermont issue. Livingston to George Clinton Sir [May 19, 1780]' I wrote to your Excellency yesterday. I trouble you again about matters of a private nature if those can be called so in which the honor & justice of the publick are materialy interested. On the last assessment I was rated at £3300. By a Letter reed, yesterday from my Agent I am informed that I am again rated at £9000 & that the whole of my income notwithstanding the most assiduous endeavour to collect it does not amount to 600 Bushls. of wheat. I have no personal property & occupy a small farm which does not half pay the expence of cultivating it.
