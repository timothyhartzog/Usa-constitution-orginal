South Carolina Delegates to John Ross Sir, [December 21? 1781] 1 Please pay Messrs. Stewart & Totten Fifty Nine Pounds 14s 7 1/2 Pensylvania Currency for so much advanced by them to pay the freight of Eight Bales of Merchandize imported from France for the State of So. Carolina, & charge the same to Acct, of said State. 2 Jno. Mathews 3 Tho. Bee A. Middleton Isaac Motte Ns. Eveleigh RC (NNS). Endorsed: “Recvd Deer 21. 1781. the within sum of fifty nine pounds 14/7 1/2 in full for Stewart & Totten. Benj. Morris.” Also endorsed by another hand: “£59.14.7 1/2 paid by order on Accot of the State of So Carolina—21 Decemr. 1781. No 3.” 1 See endorsements. 2 This day the South Carolina delegates also wrote a brief note to state agent Ross asking him at “four months after date please to pay to Dr. Budd or his order Two Hundred Silver Dollars,” which according to his endorsement, Budd received on January 8, 1782. Middendorf Deposit, MdHi. On December 24 the delegates also wrote two notes to Ross asking him to pay “Twenty Spanish milled Dollars” each to Thomas Smith and William Stone, respectively, pilots “detained here by Order of Governor Rutledge." Fogg Collection, MeHi, and C. C. Jones Collection, NNPM. See also South Carolina Delegates to Ross, December 29, 1781. 3 The delegates’ letters of this day were the last signed by John Mathews, who returned almost immediately to South Carolina in answer to an appeal from Gov. John Rutledge who had set January 8 as the date for the newly-elected assembly to convene and stipulated that “the presence of one of our delegates, as soon as the House meets, is absolutely necessary.” It was this assembly that elected Mathews governor on January 29, 1782.
