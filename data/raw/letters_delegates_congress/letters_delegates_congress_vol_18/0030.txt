Following his un cle’s death in October 1775, Randolph returned home to become the youngest member of the Virginia convention that adopted the state’s new constitution, under which he became attorney general. Ran dolph was elected to Congress in 1779, 1781, and 1782, but from time to time had to cut short his congressional activities to return to his duties in Virginia. He was later elected governor in 1786 and a delegate to the convention that drafted the federal constitution in 1787, a document which he refused to sign. He was appointed Washington’s first attorney general before eventually succeeding Jefferson as secretary of state in 1794. In Congress Randolph sup ported the 5 percent impost, conspicuously championed Virginia interest concerning western lands and navigation of the Mississippi River, urged measures to blunt the southern incursions of Lord Cornwallis, and later wrote a History of Virginia that remains an important source for the study of early American history. William Floyd 236 Floyd (1734—1821), a native of Long Island, was a general of Suffolk County militia and fled with his family to Connecticut when the British occupied New York in 1776. He was a delegate to Con gress in 1774—76, a signer of the Declaration of Independence, and a member of the New York Senate, 1777—88. He returned to Con gress intermittently from 1779 to 1783, serving on numerous com mittees and as a member of the boards of both admiralty and treas ury. He was a proponent of New York’s interests against Vermont, and he was also conspicuously concerned about Connecticut’s autho rization of raids against Long Island resulting in the indiscriminate confiscation of the property of patriots and loyalists alike, which New York protested vigorously to Congress in July 1781. Floyd was a member of the first Federal Congress, 1789—91, but was defeated in his bid for reelection, as well as in his candidacy for the post of New York lieutenant governor in 1795. He was a presidential elector in 1792, 1800, and 1804. At the age of 69 he moved to lands on the headwaters of the Mohawk River and lived out his years as a pioneer developer of a tract that later was included in Oneida County. Floyd’s sister Charity was married to his fellow New York delegate Ezra L’Hommedieu, and his daughter Catherine was briefly en gaged in 1783 to James Madison. Painting by Ralph Earl.
