486 May 3, 1782 Charles Thomson to Daniel Clark Sir May 3. 1782. I beg leave to transmit to your care and address two passports one for the Ship Fame and the other for the ship New York, which are sent from New York to carry away tobacco for Goods sold under the capitulation of Yorktown. 1 The persons interested have proved to my satisfaction that they have sold goods under the said capitulation to the amount of forty four thousand and thirty seven Spanish milled dollars and two thirds of a dollar. But as it cannot be ascertained how many hhds each of the vessels will carry I have left a blank for the number, which I have to request the favour of you to fill up & send me an Account of the quantity shipped in each Vessel. You will be pleased to procure documents to shew that the quantity shipped does not amount to more than the sum above mentioned, & that the same belong to the traders capitu- lants at York & no others. 2 As no return has been made to me of the crew of the ship New York I must farther request the favour of you to have their names inserted in the margin of the Passport & send me a list of them. I am, Sr, Your obedient humble Servt, Cha Thomson RC (DNA: PCC, item 20). 1 For discussion of the terms of the Yorktown capitulation allowing British merchants to sell their goods and export the returns in Virginia tobacco, which was purchased and exported under agreements arranged by Robert Morris with Clark and George Eddy, see Morris, Papers (Ferguson), 3:208—10, 4:404—7, 554—56. 2 Clark’s July 10 response to Thomson pledging to procure the documents requested is in PCC, item 20, 2:301-2, along with a number of other related docu ments assembled by a committee of Congress after the Virginia Assembly protested in December 1782 that the tobacco exported to New York under the protection of these passports greatly exceeded the quantity agreed upon to fulfill the terms of the Yorktown capitulation. For this and other aspects of the case, see also JCC, 24:121- 23; Morris, Papers (Ferguson), 4:143—44, 167, 367, 5:93—94, 175—76, 287—96, 330—31, 490-91, 554, 585-86, 6:177, 418-19, 644-45; and James Madison to Edmund Ran dolph, May 29, 1782.
