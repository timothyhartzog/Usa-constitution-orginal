For Congress’ seeming inability to redress the long-standing economic grievances of the general officers of the Continental Army, see these Letters, 15:572-73. 2 This day a letter was also written to the chevalier de La Luzerne by a committee of Congress—in response to a memorial from the French minister of September 24 that was read in Congress and referred to committee the following day—which is printed in JCC, 21:1011 with Secretary Thomson’s journal entry of September 25. However, the letter, which was signed by Daniel Carroll for the committee, is misdated Septem ber 25 in the journals and is erroneously printed over the signature of President Thomas McKean in both Sparks, Diplomatic Correspondence, 11:19, and Wharton, Diplomatic Correspondence, 4:728—29. The letter concerns a petition of Jahleel Smith, master of the schooner Sally, for losses arising from the Spanish capture of Pensacola, which had been brought before Congress by La Luzerne acting on behalf of the Spanish agent Francisco Rend6n. A Spanish translation of Carroll’s letter to La Luzerne, dated September 29, which Rend6n sent to Madrid with his October 27 despatch to the minister of the Indies, Josd de Galvez, is in Indiferente General, legajo 1606, Archivo General de Indias, Seville. Both Rend6n’s explanation of the case to the French minister and La Luzerne’s memorial to Congress are also printed in JCC, 21:1009—10.
