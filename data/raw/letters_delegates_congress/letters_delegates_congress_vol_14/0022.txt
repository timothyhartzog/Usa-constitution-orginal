The invasion of the state in December 1778 had thrown civil government into chaos and obstructed the routine election of delegates. The credentials of delegates chosen in 1778 had expired early in 1779, and although as many as three slates of delegates were appointed by rump conventions, councils, and assemblies during the year, none ever reached Philadelphia. The state was not again represented in Congress until May 15, 1780, when three of the five delegates elected by the Georgia assembly in January 1780 presented their credentials.
