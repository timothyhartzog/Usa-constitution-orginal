468 March 31, 1784 Committee of Congress Draft Resolve [March 31, 1784] The Committee consisting of Mr Williamson, Mr Lee & Mr Osgood to whom was referd the memorial of J Etwein, A Hubner & H C de Schweiniz 1 submit the following Resolve That in disposing of the Lands on the West Side of the Ohio, so much of the Lands adjoining to the Towns of Gnadenhutten, Schoen- brun and Salem on Muskingum be reserved for the sole use of the Christian Indians who were formerly settled there or to the Remains of that Society as may be sufficient for them to cultivate, together with all the Buildings & Improvements thereon. MS (DLC: Peter Force Miscellaneous Manuscripts). In the hand of Hugh Williamson. Endorsed: “Report of the Petition of Moravian Indians." Comee. Mr Williamson, Mr Lee, Mr Osgood, delivered March 31, 1784. Read. May 18 [1785] taken into the Ordi nance for disposing of Western lands.” 1 This October 28, 1783, memorial from Andrew Hubner, Hans Christian De Schweiniz, and John Ettwein, the Moravian bishop at Bethlehem, Pa., is in PCC, item 41, 3:73-76. It contained a request for information on the congressionally-ordered in quiry into the massacre of Moravian Indians at Gnadenhutten in March 1782 (see these Letters, 18:448), and expressed concern that the lands of the survivors were in jeopardy and ought not be opened to settlement before their claims had been purchased. It had been referred to this committee on November 1, 1783. JCC, 25:794n.3. For the “Ordi nance for disposing of Western lands” referred to in the endorsement, into which the committee’s recommendation was ultimately incorporated on May 18, 1785, and adopted by Congress two days later, see JCC, 26:324—30, 28:381. See also Charles Thom son to John Ettwein, April 7, 1784. Thomas Jefferson to George Washington Dear Sir Annapolis. Mar. 31. 1784. Your servant delivered me your favor this morning; 1 Capt. Barney is gone to Philadelphia and his vessel to Baltimore, having left with me one of your packages only. The persons who brought this could give me no certain account of the other package which you suppose to have been brought. This your servant now receives. Being obliged to seize a moment in Congress of writing you these few lines, I can only mention to you that late advices from Europe mention another revolution in the British ministry, Mr. Pitt & his friends having resigned. No new ministry was formed. This does not come however authentically. I am with very great respect & esteem Dr. Sir, Your most obedt. & most humble servt., Th. Jefferson
