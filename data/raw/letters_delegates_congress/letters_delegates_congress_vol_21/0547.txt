His attachment to the interest of the Southern States may I think be rely’d on in every instance where they clash with the Middle & East ern ones. The “temporary boundary” established in 1782, for which see these Letters, 18:320n.2, and Madison, Papers (Hutchinson), 4:184-87, had brought seri ous complaints from settlers in the region and governorsjohn Dickinson and Benjamin Harrison sought to protect each state’s interest. The commissioners from Pennsylvania (surveyor general John Lukens, Rev. John Ewing, David Rittenhouse and Thomas Hutchins) and those appointed by Virginia (Rev. James Madison, Rev. Robert Andrews, John Page and Andrew Ellicott) split into two teams establishing astronomical observa tories at the eastern end of the Mason-Dixon line near Wilmington, Del., and at its mountainous western terminus from which they made independent observations for nearly seven weeks from early July to the autumnal equinox. Comparing their results through the fall, they determined the southwest corner of Pennsylvania and issued a joint report in November 1784. Rittenhouse and Col. Andrew Porter of Pennsylvania and Ellicott and Joseph Neville of Virginia met again the following summer to run the meridian line to the Ohio River, issuing a final report in August 1785. See Pa.
