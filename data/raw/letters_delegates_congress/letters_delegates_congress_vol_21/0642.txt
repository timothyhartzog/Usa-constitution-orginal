Addressed: ‘Thomas Hutchins Esqr, Geographer to the US, Philadelphia.” 1 Williamson is apparently referring to Col. George Morgan’s efforts in behalf of New Jersey to obtain a congressional hearing for the Indiana Company’s claims against Vir ginia. Morgan’s February 26 petition on this subject had been tabled by Congress, and a March 1 motion offered by Williamson “for the appointment of a court, under the ninth of the Articles of Confederation” to hear this claim had been supported by only three states. See JCC, 26:110-12; and the April 4 postscript of Samuel Osgood to El bridge Gerry, April 3, 1784. 2 Hutchins had obviously renewed his memorial of September 8, 1783, in which he had charged that Congress had violated the “mutual agreement” reached between them in July 1781 granting him “four Dollars and four Rations per Day” as geographer to the United States by passing a November 1782 resolution reducing that allowance by half. His memorial had been referred on September 15, 1783, to a committee chaired by Williamson which recommended on October 20 that Congress should reinstate the original pay “from the time of his accepting his commission to this date and no longer, the act of 12th of November notwithstanding.” Congress, however, had taken no action on the committee’s report.
