July 17,1784 727 Samuel Hardy to Benjamin Harrison Sir, Annapolis 17th July 1784 Your Excellencys favour of the 2nd of July with its enclosures I have received. The Acts of the Virga Assembly I shall have registered on the Journals of Congress, 1 and your request that application be made to the Secretary at War for the Muster Rolls of the Virga troops shall be attended to. 2 A letter from our Ministers in Europe of the 16th of April this Morning came to hand. The only syllable of intelligence it contains is a letter from Mr Hartley informing them he had received Orders to exchange the ratification of the definitive Treaty, & should repair to Paris for that purpose as early as possible. 3 I am with great respect, Your Excellencys Most Obedt. humble ser vant. S Hardy 4 RC (Vi: Continental Congress Papers). 1 The May 3 acts of the Virginia assembly investing Congress with additional but lim ited powers to regulate commerce and authorizing the Virginia delegates to ratify an amendment to the 8th Article of Confederation were received and noted in the jour nals on July 16. See JCC, 27:289; PCC, item 75, fols. 395-96, 401-2, item 185, 3:103-4; and Hening, Statutes, 11:388-89, 401-2. The assembly resolutions of May 19 declaring the state’s intention of supporting Congress in whatever steps were necessary to close the accounts between the United States and individual states in event of the “failure of the proposition for changing the eighth Article” were entered on the journals in full the same day. See JCC, 27:589-91; and PCC, item 75, fols. 397-400. 2 For the Committee of the States’July 21 direction to the secretary in the War Office to furnish Harrison with “authenticated copies” of muster rolls of all Virginia troops that served during the war, see JCC, 27:592-93. 3 For the April 16 letter from Benjamin Franklin and John Jay to Thomas Mifflin en closing a copy of David Hartley’s April 9 letter to them (which had been received July 16 and read July 26), see PCC, item 85, fols. 432-36, item 185, 3:103.4; and Diplomatic Correspondence, 1783-89, 1:378-79. 4 This day Hardy also wrote the following brief letter to Gov. Alexander Martin of North Carolina: “In conformity to the direction of the Committee of the States, I do myself the honor to acknowledge the receit of you Excellencies favour. The acts of the General assembly of North Carolina are registered on the journals of the Committee and filed among the papers of Congress.” Governors’ Letterbooks, Nc-Ar. For the seven North Carolina assembly acts “registered on the journals,” see Francis Dana to the Mas sachusetts General Court, July 15, note.
