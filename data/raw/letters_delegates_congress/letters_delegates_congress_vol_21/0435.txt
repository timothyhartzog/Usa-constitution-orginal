This information had actually been transmitted in Franklin’s letter of November 1, which had been read in Congress on February 24 when that portion relat ing to a Canadian merchant’s accounts was referred to a committee chaired by John Beatty. A duplicate of Franklin’s letter arrived in the March 5 packet and Charles Thom son chose not to duplicate its arrival in his dispatch book, but noted its assignment on that date to a committee chaired by Elbridge Gerry. See PCC, item 82, 3:223-26, item 185, 3:94, item 186, fols. 150-51. A close examination of the RC indicates that the entire letter was written at one sitting, undoubtedly on March 1. Franklin had assured Congress of the continued goodwill of France toward the United States despite contrary rumors. “The late failure of payment in the Caisse d’Escompte,’’ he explained, “an institution similar to the Bank of England, occasioned partly by its having gone too far in assisting the government with money, and the inability of the government to support their credit, though extremely desirous of doing it, is a fresh proof that our not ob taining a further loan was not occasioned by want of good will to assist us, as some have un justly supposed, but by a real want of the means.” Wharton, Diplomatic Correspondence, 6:721.
