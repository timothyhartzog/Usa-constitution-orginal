John Montgomery to Edward Hand Dear Sir Annoplas 12th Febry 1784 The inclosed Letter Came by the Post and which I now Send to you. 1 Thire is nothing done in Congrass Since you left us. We are now to Chuse a Deputy Secrty for Charles Thomson. Humphreys and Emson are also in nomination for this office so that Humphreys Stand a prity good Chance for one of them. I think that Emson will be apointed to the Latter and Humphreys to the former. 2 Too day we were to have apointed but thire was not a Congrass. Shall be glad to hear from you. I am Dear Sir your assured Frind and Humbl Servt, John Montgomery RC (PHarH: Hand Papers). Addressed: “Honble Edward Hand at Lanchester.” 1 Not identified. 2 Montgomery’s writing is opaque, but Congress had resolved on February 3, before Hand’s departure, to appoint an under secretary for the department of foreign affairs, a decision postponed on the 6th, and on the 10th and 11th, when Congress also or dered ‘That to-morrow be assigned for electing a deputy secretary” for Charles Thom son. Congress eventually took up both issues on the 24th and 25th without reaching a decision, but finally elected Henry Remsen, Jr., under secretary in the office for foreign affairs on March 2. However, the post of deputy secretary to Thomson, which had been left vacant at the resignation of George Bond on October 31, 1783, was not filled until June 23, 1785, when Roger Alden was elected. See JCC, 25:771, 26:64-65, 70, 74, 77, 93, 122, 28:474; and PCC, item 18A, fols. 160-66. “Emson” is apparently a reference to Henry Remsen. David Humphreys, a former Washington aide who had recently been recommended by the general to Congress for employment, was eventually elected on May 12 to be “Secretary to the Commission for negotiating treaties of Commerce with foreign powers.” See JCC, 27:375; PCC, item 78, 12:279-82; and Jacob Read to Washing ton, February 1, 1784, note 3.
