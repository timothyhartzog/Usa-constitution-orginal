Elias Boudinot to Thomas Mifflin Dear Sir, Elizabeth Town Novr 21. 1783 Last Evening I reed, the enclosed Letters & Treaty from New York, brought there by the Packett from England. 1 Not knowing from the direction but that they were private Letters, I opened them and for ward them on by the first opportunity. I paid a Charge of 17/11 Postage by the Packett. The British have at last evacuated New York and the Flag of the united States is now flying on the Fort of that City. 2 I have the honor to be with very great respect, Your Excellency’s Most Obed Hble Serv Elias Boudinot RC (DNA: PCC, item 78). Endorsed by Charles Thomson: “Letter 21 Novr 1783. E Boudinot, enclosing two letters from doctr Franklin & a copy of definitive treaty.” 1 The enclosed August 31 and September 13 letters from Benjamin Franklin to ex president Boudinot are in PCC, item 82, 2:73-88. 2 Boudinot was mistaken, for American troops did not occupy New York until Novem ber 25 following the withdrawal of the last British troops from the city the same day, a fact General Washington reported to Congress on December 3d. In a previous letter of November 18, Washington had sent Congress a copy of Sir Guy Carleton’s November 12 letter to him explaining that the evacuation would probably be completed “some days before the end of the present month," and that he expected to get his troops off Man hattan and Long Island on November 21 and 22, “and Staten Island as soon after as may be practicable.” See PCC, item 152, 11:515-25; and Washington, Writings (Fitz patrick), 27:246, 255. Elbridge Gerry to John Adams My dear Mr Adams Philadelphia 23d Novr 83. (Sunday Even) Mr Thaxter arrived here last Evening, by the Way of New York with the definitive Treaty, 1 having narrowly escaped a severe storm by reaching that Port on Wednesday Evening. Your Favours by him I have received with great Pleasure, 2 as I was in Want of the Information they contain, as well as of your Sentiments on several important Subjects. Governor Reed will probably deliver You this, & my Confidence in him will induce me to be explicit.
