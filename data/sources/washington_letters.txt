Monday, May 28. The Convention assembled. Mr. Washington was unanimously elected President, and Mr. Jacob Morris appointed Secretary. A committee to prepare the rules of order was appointed.

Tuesday, May 29. Mr. Virginia presented a set of resolutions which formed the basis for the discussions to follow. The principal propositions were: 1. That the national government ought to consist of three branches. 2. That the first branch should be elected by the people of the several states. 3. That the second branch be elected by the first branch. 4. That the executive be chosen by the national legislature. The debate proceeded on the question whether the legislative branch should consist of one or two chambers.

Wednesday, May 30. The convention continued debating the structure of the legislature. Mr. Washington argued that a single legislative chamber would be unwise, as it would be subject to hasty and violent measures. Others contended that a bicameral legislature would lead to complexity and delay. The discussion of state representation continued.Monday, May 28. The Convention assembled.
