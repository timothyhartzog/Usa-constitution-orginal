Monday, May 28. The Convention assembled. Mr. Washington was unanimously elected President, and Mr. Jacob Morris appointed Secretary. A committee to prepare the rules of order was appointed.

Tuesday, May 29. Mr. Virginia presented a set of resolutions which formed the basis for the discussions to follow. The principal propositions were: 1. That the national government ought to consist of three branches. 2. That the first branch should be elected by the people of the several states. 3. That the second branch be elected by the first branch. 4. That the executive be chosen by the national legislature. The debate proceeded on the question whether the legislative branch should consist of one or two chambers.

Wednesday, May 30. The convention continued debating the structure of the legislature. Mr. Madison argued that a single legislative chamber would be unwise, as it would be subject to hasty and violent measures. Others contended that a bicameral legislature would lead to complexity and delay. The discussion of state representation continued.FEDERALIST NO. 10 - JAMES MADISON Among the numerous advantages promised by a well-constructed union, none deserves to be more accurately developed than its tendency to break and control the violence of faction. The friend of popular governments never finds himself so much alarmed for their character and fate as when he contemplates their propensity to this dangerous vice. He will not fail, therefore, to set a due value on any plan which, without violating the principles to which he is attached, provides a proper cure for it.

By faction I understand a number of citizens, whether amounting to a minority or majority of the whole, who are united and actuated by some common impulse of passion, or of interest, adverse to the rights of other citizens. There are two methods of curing the mischiefs of faction: the one, by removing its causes; the other, by controlling its effects.

FEDERALIST NO. 51 - JAMES MADISON To what expedient, then, shall we finally resort, for maintaining in practice the necessary partition of power among the several departments as laid down in the constitution? The answer that suggests itself is that as the exterior provision is first to be made by so contriving the interior structure of the government as that its several constituent parts may, by their mutual relations, be the means of keeping each other in their proper places.Monday, May 28.
