## Detailed Sources

### 1. U.S. Constitution (1787)

**Primary Source:** Constitutional Convention, September 17, 1787

**Download Sources:**
- **National Archives** (Official): https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/constitution-transcript
- **Wikisource**: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_United_States_of_America
- **Project Gutenberg**: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/5000

**Public Domain Status:** Public domain (government document)

**Format:** Plain text + HTML

**Chunks:** ~12 chunks (split by Article)

**Notes:** All three sources have minor formatting differences. Wikisource provides clean markup; National Archives provides official government version.

### 2. Bill of Rights (1791)

**Primary Source:** First Congress of the United States, December 15, 1791

**Download Sources:**
- **National Archives** (Official): https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/bill-of-rights-transcript
- **Wikisource**: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/United_States_Bill_of_Rights

**Public Domain Status:** Public domain (government document)

**Format:** Plain text + HTML

**Chunks:** ~15 chunks (split by amendment)

### 3. Madison's Notes of the Constitutional Convention (1787)

**Primary Source:** James Madison's daily debate record, May 25 - September 17, 1787

**Published:** 1840 edition by Gideon

**Download Sources:**
- **Yale Avalon Project** (Recommended): https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/debates_toc.asp
- **Archive.org**: https://archive.org/details/journalsconventionunitedstates
- **Project Gutenberg**: Various 19th century editions

**Public Domain Status:** Public domain (published 1840)

**Format:** HTML (Avalon), OCR text (Archive.org)

**Chunks:** ~300 chunks (split by debate date/topic)

**Notes:** 
- Avalon Project version is best for structure and readability
- Archive.org has multiple editions; 1840 edition recommended
- Madison's notes are the most complete record of internal convention debates

---

### 4. Farrand's Records of the Federal Convention (3 Volumes)

**Primary Source:** Max Farrand's comprehensive compilation of convention records

**Published:** 1911 (revised edition 1937, now public domain)

**Volume Coverage:**
- **Volume 1:** May 25 - July 31, 1787
- **Volume 2:** August 1 - September 17, 1787
- **Volume 3:** Documents, indices, and appendix

**Download Sources:**
- **Archive.org** (Recommended): https://archive.org/details/recordsfederalc00farr
- **Yale Avalon Project**: https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/farrand.asp

**Public Domain Status:** Public domain (published 1911-1937)

**Format:** Full text OCR + HTML transcriptions

**Chunks:** ~600 chunks (split by session/entry)

**Notes:**
- Farrand's is the standard scholarly reference
- More comprehensive than Madison's notes alone
- Includes supplementary documents and secretary notes
- Archive.org text quality varies by volume

---

### 5. Federalist Papers (85 Essays)

**Primary Source:** Alexander Hamilton, James Madison, and John Jay essays in support of Constitution ratification

**Published:** 1787-1788 (serialized), compiled 1788-1789

**Author Attribution:**
- Hamilton: Essays 1-11, 13-36, 59-61, 65-85 (51 essays)
- Madison: Essays 10, 14, 37-58, 62-63 (29 essays)
- Jay: Essays 2-5, 64 (5 essays)
- Disputed: Essays 18-20, 49-58 (Hamilton/Madison)

**Download Sources:**
- **Project Gutenberg** (Recommended): https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/18
- **Yale Avalon Project**: https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/fed.asp
- **Constitution.org**: http://www.constitution.org/fed/federa.htm

**Public Domain Status:** Public domain (published 1788-1789)

**Format:** Plain text (Gutenberg), HTML (Avalon, Constitution.org)

**Chunks:** ~400 chunks (typically one or two per essay)

**Notes:**
- Federalist Papers are seminal documents for understanding Constitution's intent
- Project Gutenberg text is clean and properly formatted
- Essays are numbered 1-85, with standard numbering across all sources
- Complete collection essential for ratification debate context

---

### 6. Anti-Federalist Writings (Comprehensive Collection)

**Primary Source:** Essays and speeches opposing Constitution ratification (1787-1788)

**Key Authors:**
- George Mason (Virginia)
- Patrick Henry (Virginia)
- Elbridge Gerry (Massachusetts)
- Robert Yates & John Lansing (New York)
- Luther Martin (Maryland)
- George Clinton (New York)
- Samuel Adams (Massachusetts)
- Richard Henry Lee (Virginia)
- Other Anti-Federalist contributors

**Published:** 1787-1788 (contemporary) + 19th-20th century collections

**Download Sources:**
- **Yale Avalon Project** (Recommended): https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/anti.asp
- **Constitution.org**: http://www.constitution.org/afp/afp.htm
- **Archive.org**: Search "Anti-Federalist Papers"

**Public Domain Status:** Public domain (published 1787-1788)

**Format:** HTML (Avalon, Constitution.org), OCR text (Archive.org)

**Chunks:** ~450 chunks

**Notes:**
- No single comprehensive publication during ratification era
- Collected from newspapers, essays, letters, state convention records
- Avalon Project provides best coverage and structure
- Essential for understanding opposition arguments
- Includes attacks on specific Constitution provisions and concerns about centralized power

### 7. Founders' Correspondence (1786-1789)

**Primary Source:** Letters and documents among founding generation during formation period

**Key Correspondents:**
- George Washington
- James Madison  
- Alexander Hamilton
- Thomas Jefferson
- James Monroe
- John Jay
- Benjamin Franklin
- James Wilson
- James Polk
- Elbridge Gerry
- And 50+ other key figures

**Date Range:** January 1, 1786 - December 31, 1789

**Download Sources:**
- **Founders Online** (UVA, Recommended): https://founders.archives.gov/
  - Comprehensive database of all founders' writings
  - Advanced search by person, date, subject
  - Full-text searchable
  
- **Library of Congress American Memory**: https://memory.loc.gov/ammem/
  - Multiple historical collections
  - Specific founders' papers
  
- **Archive.org**: Individual founders' paper collections
  - Washington Papers: https://archive.org/details/papers-of-george-washington
  - Jefferson Papers: Multiple collections available
  - Hamilton Papers: Multiple collections available

**Public Domain Status:** Public domain (published 18th-20th centuries)

**Format:** Transcribed documents, OCR texts, scanned images

**Chunks:** ~600 chunks

**Notes:**
- Founders Online is the most comprehensive and authoritative source
- Provides context and annotations for major documents
- Access methods vary by source (HTML, plain text, images)
- Correspondence reveals debates not recorded in official convention notes
- Includes letters about ratification, implementation, and constitutional meaning

## Data Quality Notes

### OCR Accuracy
- Archive.org texts vary in OCR quality (70-95% accuracy)
- Cleaned using regex patterns to remove common OCR artifacts
- Manual review recommended for critical passages

### Completeness
- Constitutional documents are complete texts
- Convention notes and Farrand's represent complete records
- Federalist Papers: All 85 essays included
- Anti-Federalist: Comprehensive but not exhaustive collection of all contemporary critiques
- Correspondence: Sample of major figures' letters (not complete archives)

### Historical Authenticity
- Original spelling and punctuation preserved
- No modernization of language
- Editorial additions marked where applicable
